# Digest round 2 — wake architecture, screen/browser, distribution

## Findings

### Q1 — Event-driven wake, FGS-from-background exemptions, Play policy
- claim: Apps targeting Android 12+ cannot start a foreground service (FGS) from the background except in listed cases. Otherwise the system throws ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start | publisher: Google / Android Developers | pub_date: undated (fetched current) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: A high-priority FCM message is a listed exemption. Caveat: the system can downgrade high-priority messages to normal priority if the app does not use them to surface time-sensitive content to the user. A downgraded message cannot start an FGS. Docs advise checking RemoteMessage.getPriority() == PRIORITY_HIGH before starting the FGS. | source: same as above | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim: "Your app invokes an exact alarm to complete an action that the user requests" is a listed exemption. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim: "The user turns off battery optimizations for your app" is a listed exemption. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim: SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW is an exemption. For apps targeting Android 15+, the app must also currently have a visible overlay window. Other exemptions: being the current IME, user interaction with the app's notification, widget or bubble, BOOT_COMPLETED / LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED / MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED (Android 14+ target restricts some FGS types from BOOT_COMPLETED), time/locale change broadcasts, geofencing or activity-recognition events, Companion Device Manager permissions, and device or profile owner. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim: Being a bound AccessibilityService or NotificationListenerService is NOT in the list of FGS background-start exemptions. Being the current input method (IME) is listed. | source: same (absence in the full list) | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (inferred from absence) | class: capability
- claim: Even when exempt, an FGS that needs while-in-use permissions (camera, microphone, location, body sensors) cannot be created from the background (SecurityException on Android 14+). Exceptions to this include starts from a notification or widget interaction, and an app that provides the VoiceInteractionService. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: failure
- claim: Android 17 adds an AlarmManager.setExactAndAllowWhileIdle variant that takes an OnAlarmListener instead of a PendingIntent. Google pitches it for apps that currently hold continuous wakelocks for periodic tasks, for example messaging apps maintaining socket connections. | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/features | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated (Android 17 page) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim: Android 17 background audio hardening applies to all apps: playback and volume APIs fail silently, and audio focus returns AUDIOFOCUS_REQUEST_FAILED, if called outside a valid lifecycle. Apps targeting 17 need an FGS with while-in-use capabilities, unless they hold the exact alarm permission and use USAGE_ALARM streams. | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Android 17 introduces app memory limits based on device RAM. A killed app shows REASON_OTHER with "MemoryLimiter:AnonSwap" in ApplicationExitInfo. This is relevant to long-lived agent processes. | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Play policy describes USE_EXACT_ALARM as restricted: "apps must only declare this permission if their core functionality supports the need for an exact alarm." | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim (from search summary, not fetched verbatim): The acceptable USE_EXACT_ALARM use cases are alarm or timer apps and calendar apps that show event notifications. Other apps should use the user-granted SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM. Non-compliant apps are disallowed from publishing on Play. | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241 ; https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: policy
- claim: Play Accessibility API policy says the API "is not designed and cannot be requested for ... an app that autonomously initiates, plans, and executes actions or decisions." Key consideration: "Don't use the API to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions or decisions." The fetched text gives no user-initiated exception. isAccessibilityTool is reserved for apps whose core functionality directly supports people with disabilities. | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: Play is adding an AppFunctions API policy item, effective April 14, 2027: "Developers must ensure their AppFunctions do not deceive or manipulate AI agents, bypass security controls, or interfere with other applications." | source: same | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim (search summary only): A process bound with BIND_AUTO_CREATE is ranked at least as important as its binder. Accessibility services are bound only by the system and are started or stopped only through the user's Settings toggle. No primary source found stating which importance tier a bound AccessibilityService or NotificationListenerService process receives. | source: https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/memory/guide/service-bindings ; https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/processes/process-lifecycle | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: low | class: capability

### Q2 — MediaProjection limits
- claim: The app must request user consent before each media projection session. A session is a single createVirtualDisplay() call, and the token must be used only once. On Android 14+, createVirtualDisplay() throws SecurityException if the createScreenCaptureIntent() result is passed to getMediaProjection() more than once, or if createVirtualDisplay() is called twice on one MediaProjection. | source: https://developer.android.com/media/grow/media-projection | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: A session stops when the user stops it from the app UI or the status-bar chip, when the screen is locked, when another projection session starts, or when the process is killed. On Android 15 QPR1+, a large status-bar chip shows during projection, and projection auto-stops on screen lock. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: failure
- claim: Targeting Android 14+ requires FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION and an FGS of type mediaProjection. Android 14+ lets users choose to share a single app window, which excludes the status bar, notifications and system UI. | source: same | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim (implication): Because consent is per session and projection stops on screen lock, MediaProjection cannot provide unattended 24/7 screen reading. Each screen-lock cycle needs a new user consent. | source: derived from the above | confidence: high | class: failure

### Q3 — On-device browser automation
- claim: Playwright's Android support is experimental. It drives Chrome for Android or WebView from a host computer over ADB and requires Chrome 105+ plus the "Enable command line on non-rooted devices" flag. Raw USB is unsupported and the device must be awake for screenshots. It is not an on-device solution. | source: https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-android | publisher: Microsoft / Playwright | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: capability
- claim (search summary): Onslip/automation is a Node.js, Playwright-inspired library for remote-controlling Android and iOS WebViews. It also requires adb, so it is host-side. | source: https://github.com/Onslip/automation | publisher: Onslip (GitHub) | pub_date: unknown | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: low | class: capability
- claim (search summary): Inside an app, the standard primitive is WebView.evaluateJavascript (with a result callback). Jetpack JavaScriptEngine evaluates JS without a WebView but cannot drive pages. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/jsengine | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: capability

### Q4 — Android 17 AAPM and Accessibility
- claim: The primary Android 17 features page describes AAPM as an opt-in single setting that blocks sideloading (app installs from unknown sources), restricts USB data signaling, and mandates Play Protect scanning. Apps can detect it with the AdvancedProtectionManager API. The page does NOT mention restricting Accessibility to isAccessibilityTool apps, and neither does behavior-changes-all. The features page still links a "c-beta2" API diff, so it may not be fully refreshed for final. | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/features ; https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all | publisher: Android Developers | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high (for what the pages say) | class: version-compat
- claim (search summary, secondary): AAPM in Android 17 revokes AccessibilityService access for apps not flagged isAccessibilityTool="true" and prevents granting it while AAPM is on. The change first appeared in Beta 2 (March 2026). An October 2026 report describes it as part of Android 17's protections. | source: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/android-17-advanced-protection-locks.html ; https://www.thaicert.or.th/en/2026/03/17/android-17-enhances-security-by-restricting-accessibility-api-access-to-reduce-malware-risks/ | publisher: The Hacker News; ThaiCERT | pub_date: 2026-10; 2026-03-17 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: version-compat
- claim (implication): Because AAPM also blocks sideloading, users who turn it on can neither install a GitHub/F-Droid APK nor (per secondary sources) grant Accessibility to a non-a11y-tool agent. | source: derived from above | confidence: medium | class: failure
