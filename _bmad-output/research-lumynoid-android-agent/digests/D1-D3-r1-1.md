# Digest D1+D3 (runtime)

Scope note: two-pass budget (~22 tool calls, 17 distinct sources). All claims below come from pages retrieved on 2026-11-14. "pub_date" is the page's own date where one was visible. Otherwise it is "undated" (developer.android.com pages show no date in the fetched text). Vendor-sourced items are marked low/medium.

## Findings

### D1

- claim: For apps targeting Android 15+, `dataSync` and `mediaProcessing` FGS may run a combined 6 h per 24 h (tracked separately per type and shared across all of the app's services of that type). At the limit the system calls `Service.onTimeout()`, and the app must call `stopSelf()` within seconds or it crashes with RemoteServiceException. Starting another dataSync FGS after the limit throws `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException`. The timer resets only when the user brings the app to the foreground. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout | publisher: Google (Android Developers) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Google's own mitigation advice for the dataSync timeout is to start it only from direct user interaction, or to use WorkManager or user-initiated data transfer (UIDT) jobs instead. In other words, dataSync is not a valid "24/7 agent" service type. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: Android 16 (all apps, regardless of targetSdk): background jobs started from a foreground service (JobScheduler, WorkManager, DownloadManager) now must follow their normal runtime quotas. Holding an FGS no longer lifts job quotas. UIDT jobs are exempt. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/changes | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Android 15 (targetSdk 35+): BOOT_COMPLETED receivers can no longer launch certain FGS types, and the SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW exemption for background FGS start now requires a currently visible overlay window. Android 14 (targetSdk 34+) requires every FGS to declare a type plus the matching FOREGROUND_SERVICE_<TYPE> permission, otherwise SecurityException. Android 12 (targetSdk 31+) bans FGS start from the background except for listed exemptions. | source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/changes | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Android 16 target-specific changes include `scheduleAtFixedRate` running at most one missed execution when the app returns to a valid lifecycle (relevant to in-process timers in an agent loop). The Local Network Protection permission is opt-in now, with enforcement planned "at a later Android release" (25Q2–26Q2 rollout plan). That matters if the agent talks to LAN devices or hosts a LAN server. | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16 | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: Android 17 (API 37) docs are published. All-apps changes include new app memory limits based on device RAM (kills are reported as REASON_OTHER with "MemoryLimiter:AnonSwap"), background audio hardening (FGS needed; targetSdk 37 needs while-in-use FGS), and blocked cross-profile loopback traffic. No new FGS-type or timeout change for general work was listed on that page. Final release status and date for Android 17 were not confirmed (a news result says background audio hardening shipped with Beta 4). | source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all | publisher: Google | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (release status unverified) | class: version-compat
- claim: Play policy (Device and Network Abuse): for targetSdk 34+, an FGS permission may be declared only if the use is user-beneficial and core to the app, user-initiated or user-perceptible, stoppable by the user, not deferrable without harming the UX, and "runs only as long as needed to complete the task". systemExempted and shortService are exempt. An open-ended always-on agent is a poor fit for the last criterion. | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16273414 | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: The Play Console requires a per-FGS-type declaration with a description, the user impact if deferred, a video showing the user steps that trigger it, and a use case. specialUse is allowed only "in limited scenarios" that meet the general FGS criteria. All types are reviewed. dataSync videos cannot show fully automatic or background work, and "routine system- or server-initiated tasks under dataSync" are listed as unacceptable. | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821 | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated (mentions 2023-11 to 2025-01 milestones) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: dontkillmyapp.com still ranks Huawei, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Samsung at the worst severity (5/5), Meizu, Asus, Oppo and Ulefone at 4, Vivo, realme, Motorola, Lenovo and others at 3, and Pixel/AOSP, Nokia and HTC at 0. - claim: The Xiaomi page covers MIUI 10–14 only and does not mention HyperOS. ### D3

- claim: Android 10+ W^X: for targetSdk 29+, "Execution of files from the writable app home directory is a W^X violation". Apps should exec only binary code embedded in the APK. The F-Droid/GitHub Termux therefore stays on targetSdk 28 ("Termux does not support Android 10 officially"), and the wiki says Play updates stopped for that reason. | source: https://github.com/termux/termux-packages/wiki/Termux-and-Android-10 | publisher: Termux (GitHub wiki) | pub_date: 2024-05-26 (last edit) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: version-compat
- claim: A separate Termux Play Store build (termux-play-store org) returned to Play in June 2024 with targetSdk bumped to current. It works around exec restrictions with a system_linker_exec approach (exec via /system/bin/linker64) and needed fixes for packages that rely on /proc/self/exe. A 2026.01.07 Play release is reported. The README labels the Play build "Experimental branch… missing functionality and bugs". Evidence comes from a search summary of termux-play-store and discussion #4000 plus the main README. Detailed mechanics were not fetched first-hand. | source: https://github.com/termux-play-store ; https://github.com/termux/termux-app/discussions/4000 ; https://github.com/termux/termux-app | publisher: Termux | pub_date: 2024-06 to 2026-01 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: version-compat
- claim: Play policy: apps may not download executable code (dex, JAR, .so) from outside Google Play, nor self-update outside Play. Code running in a VM or interpreter with indirect Android API access (e.g. JavaScript in a WebView) is excluded, but interpreted code (JS, Python, Lua) loaded at runtime "must not allow potential policy violations". Implication: a Play build can run agent-generated JS or Python in an embedded interpreter, but downloading native toolchains (node binaries, git, etc.) at runtime breaches policy. Sideloaded/F-Droid builds are not bound by this. | source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16273414 | publisher: Google Play Console Help | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: policy
- claim: OpenCode has no official Android build. `npm i -g opencode-ai` in Termux fails because postinstall looks for a nonexistent `opencode-android-arm64` package. Issue #12515 (opened 2026-02-06) got no maintainer response or fix and was auto-closed as stale on 2026-04-11. Duplicates #11689 (Android aarch64 build request) and #10504 exist. | source: https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/12515 | publisher: OpenCode (GitHub) | pub_date: 2026-02-06 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: failure
- claim: Community workarounds for OpenCode on Termux exist. Hope2333/opencode-termux wraps the upstream Linux arm64 binary via bun-termux-loader (needs glibc) and ships deb/pacman packages, with active 2026 releases (Push260522, Push260719). Other routes are a precompiled native build (criollojoel10, OpenCode 1.14.25) and running inside proot-distro. | source: https://github.com/HanSoBored/opencode-termux ; https://newreleases.io/project/github/Hope2333/opencode-termux/release/Push260719 ; https://jonathansblog.co.uk/running-opencode-in-termux-on-android | publisher: community | pub_date: 2026-05 to 2026-07 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (search summaries, not fetched individually) | class: ecosystem
- claim: Claude Code on Termux has reportedly been broken natively since v2.1.113 (2026-04-17), when it moved to a precompiled binary. Termux reports platform "android", and the `claude-code-linux-arm64-android` package 404s. Tracking issue anthropics/claude-code #50270 was reportedly still open on 2026-10-30. Workarounds: pin 2.1.112, use glibc-runner, or use proot Ubuntu. Codex CLI has no native Android build, and its bundled musl Rust binary needs a community workaround. | source: https://cosyra.com/guides/claude-code-termux-fix.html ; https://cosyra.com/guides/codex-cli-on-android.html | publisher: Cosyra (vendor of a cloud alternative; potential bias) | pub_date: 2026-10 (claimed check 2026-10-30) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: low-medium (single vendor source; issue not fetched) | class: failure
- claim: Pattern across 2026 CLI coding agents: the move to precompiled Bun/Rust single binaries (OpenCode, Claude Code, Codex) has made Termux (Bionic libc, platform=android) a second-class target. The working paths are a glibc shim (glibc-runner / bun-termux-loader) or a full proot Linux userland. | source: https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/12515 ; https://cosyra.com/guides/claude-code-termux-fix.html | publisher: OpenCode GitHub; Cosyra | pub_date: 2026-02 / 2026-10 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: ecosystem
- claim: Chaquopy (Python embedded in Android apps, open source since 12.0.1 in 2022) is actively maintained. 17.2.0 shipped 2026-10-26 (AGP 9.5 support, pip 26.2.2) and 17.1.0 on 2026-10-12 (adds Python 3.15). It supports Python 3.10–3.15, defaults to 3.10, and needs min API 24. 17.1.0 "makes .so files read-only before loading", consistent with W^X-era constraints. | source: https://chaquo.com/chaquopy/doc/current/changelog.html | publisher: Chaquo (official changelog) | pub_date: 2026-10-26 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: ecosystem

## Leads (worth chasing, not chased)
- termux-play-store: the actual targetSdk and exact system_linker_exec mechanism, plus the list of packages or features missing vs F-Droid (discussion #4000 and the termux-play-store org README). This decides whether an embedded Termux-like userland can ship on Play.
- anthropics/claude-code issue #50270: verify open/closed status directly (only a vendor source so far).
- Android 16/17 Doze, App Standby bucket, exact alarm (SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM / USE_EXACT_ALARM Play policy) and JobScheduler quota pages were not fetched. Primary docs needed for an event-driven design.
- FGS background-start exemptions list (restrictions-bg-start page). In particular, whether high-priority FCM, exact alarms, or REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS exempt starting an FGS. This is the core of a "wake on FCM/schedule" architecture.
- Play policy on REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS (acceptable use cases).
- dontkillmyapp HyperOS / Samsung One UI 7–8 / ColorOS 15 currency; there may be newer OEM-specific sources.
- Apps that embed a proot userland without Termux (UserLAnd, AnLinux, Andronix status in 2026), and whether any are on Play with a current targetSdk.
- Android 17 final release date and whether AVF/Linux Terminal (Debian VM on Pixel) is available to third-party apps. That could be an alternative execution sandbox.
- Hope2333/opencode-termux and guysoft/opencode-termux repos directly: stability and Android 16 compatibility.
