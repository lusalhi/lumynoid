# Digest D4+D5 (LLM access + landscape)

Research run: 2026-11-14. All findings from web search / web fetch in this run. Tool budget: ~21 calls, ~17 distinct primary/secondary sources fetched or cited. Secondary-aggregator claims are marked low/medium.

## Findings

### D4 — Free LLM access for an agent

**OpenRouter**
- claim: OpenRouter `:free` models are limited to 20 requests/minute for every account. The daily cap is 50 requests/day for accounts that have bought less than 10 credits all-time and 1000 requests/day for accounts that have bought at least 10 credits. The page says the higher cap starts one credit below the threshold, so at 9 credits. | source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits | publisher: OpenRouter (official docs) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: pricing-limits
- claim: OpenRouter exposes `free_model_daily_requests` (used/limit/remaining, current UTC day) through `GET /api/v1/key`, but not the per-minute limit. BYOK requests are not counted against the free-model limit. | source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits | publisher: OpenRouter | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: pricing-limits
- claim: People report that tool calling on OpenRouter free models is unreliable: some calls fail with "No endpoints found that support tool use" and some return empty tool calls. One developer found gpt-oss-20b:free the most reliable free option in a fallback chain. OpenRouter's official advice is to filter models with `supported_parameters=tools`. | source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/tool-calling ; https://community.n8n.io/t/tool-calling-bug-for-agents-using-openrouter-various-models/295318 ; https://dev.to/mrclaw207/i-wired-openrouter-free-models-into-my-openclaw-fallback-chain-heres-what-actually-works-580f | publisher: OpenRouter docs + n8n forum + DEV blog | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (anecdotal; no controlled benchmark) | class: capability

**OpenCode Zen / OpenCode free models**

Most are free only "for a limited time", with no end date given. LongCat 2.5 Free is capped at 100 requests/day. Two free models are being deprecated: Pulse Alpha Free (2026-11-06) and Crouton Alpha Free (2026-11-09). | source: https://opencode.ai/docs/zen/ | publisher: OpenCode (official docs) | pub_date: undated (deprecation dates 2026-11) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: pricing-limits
- claim: The free chat models require OpenCode, and other clients cannot use them. A third-party app such as Lumynoid probably cannot call Zen free models directly. Some free models are also geo-restricted (for example, Inkling 2 Small Free is unavailable in the EU). | source: https://opencode.ai/docs/zen/ | publisher: OpenCode | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high (as stated in docs; enforcement mechanism not verified) | class: pricing-limits
- claim: Privacy terms on Zen free models differ by model. Data may be used for training or retained on Big Pickle, Iceman Free, Inkling 2 Small Free, Flint 40B Free (30-day retention) and MiMo-V2.6-Flash Free. The NVIDIA Nemotron free models carry the warning "do not submit personal or confidential data". GLM/DeepSeek/MiniMax Flash Free are listed as zero-retention. | source: https://opencode.ai/docs/zen/ | publisher: OpenCode | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: privacy

**On-device fallback**
- claim: The ML Kit GenAI Prompt API (`com.google.mlkit:genai-prompt`, Gemini Nano) is documented for third-party Android apps but is in **beta** ("not subject to any SLA or deprecation policy"). `GenerativeModel` does not support tool calling directly; multi-step tool execution is described only for a separate `AgentRunner` class in the "agentic workflows" docs. The page does not state supported devices or quotas. | source: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/prompt/android | publisher: Google (official) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high (status), medium (AgentRunner scope not verified) | class: capability
- claim: Google's LiteRT-LM lists "function calling support for agentic workflows with constrained decoding" and has a Kotlin/Android guide. The README notes improved function calling in v0.9.0. | source: https://ai.google.dev/edge/litert-lm/overview | publisher: Google AI Edge (official) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (seen via search snippet, page not fully fetched) | class: capability
- claim: Small on-device tool-calling models are limited. The FunctionGemma 270M LiteRT-LM model card says it does not support multi-step chaining (single or parallel calls only). That is too weak for agent loops; it fits intent→single-action routing. | source: https://huggingface.co/soniqo/FunctionGemma-270M-LiteRT-LM | publisher: Hugging Face model card (community conversion) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: capability
- claim: Operit already ships on-device inference on Android through MNN and GGUF/llama.cpp alongside cloud BYOK. AidanPark's openclaw-android guide calls local LLM inference "impractical on most phones" because of RAM, storage and CPU limits. | source: https://github.com/AAswordman/Operit ; https://github.com/AidanPark/openclaw-android | publisher: GitHub repos | pub_date: Operit v1.12.0 2026-07-01 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: capability

**Consumption vs quotas**
- claim: In a 2026 paper, CLI-based mobile agents averaged 10.7 steps per task versus 18.6 for GUI (screen-driven) baselines, and the CLI agents also scored higher in every category. Each step is roughly one LLM call, so a GUI phone task uses about 10–20 calls. | source: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19388.md | publisher: arXiv/alphaXiv preprint "Beyond the GUI Paradigm" | pub_date: 2026-06 | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (search snippet; paper not fully read) | class: capability
- claim: Agent token use varies widely. For agentic coding, runs on the same task differ by up to 30x in total tokens (Stanford DEI). Because context accumulates, cost grows faster than step count, and one unverified estimate puts a 50-step Android automation run at about 250K tokens before trimming the screen state (about 25–40K after). | source: https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/how-do-ai-agents-spend-your-money ; https://dev.to/elliotgao2/stop-wasting-tokens-on-android-automation-1mep | publisher: Stanford Digital Economy Lab; DEV blog | pub_date: 2026-05 (Stanford); undated (DEV) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (Stanford), low (DEV estimate) | class: capability
- claim (derived): At 10–20 calls per phone task, OpenRouter's 50 RPD (no credits) covers roughly 2–5 tasks a day, and 1000 RPD (≥10 credits bought) covers roughly 50–100. Groq Developer at 200K TPD would be used up by one or two 250K-token unoptimized runs. Running free models 24/7 needs aggressive context trimming plus key and provider rotation. | source: derived from the OpenRouter, Groq and alphaXiv/DEV findings above | publisher: — | pub_date: — | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: low (arithmetic on uncertain inputs) | class: pricing-limits

### D5 — Existing alternatives

- claim: **DroidRun (now branded Mobilerun)** is an MIT-licensed framework for LLM agents that control Android and iOS devices (reading UI state and screenshots, then tapping, swiping and typing). It supports BYOK with OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, xAI, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, OpenAI-compatible models and Ollama, and has about 10.0k stars. It runs on a PC (Python 3.11–3.13) and drives the phone over ADB or the Portal app, so it does not run fully on the phone. There is also a hosted Mobilerun Cloud. | source: https://github.com/droidrun/droidrun | publisher: GitHub (official repo) | pub_date: undated (no releases listed) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: landscape
- claim: **Operit** (AAswordman) is an LGPL-3.0 Android-native agent platform with about 9.6k stars. Latest release v1.12.0 (2026-07-01). It is BYOK (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini and compatible APIs, with key pools) and also runs local models (MNN, llama.cpp GGUF, Ollama/LM Studio). It has an on-phone Ubuntu 24.04 ARM64 terminal through PRoot, tools/MCP/Skills/workflows, and device automation through Accessibility, Shizuku or Root. It runs on the phone itself, but requires an ARM64 device on Android 8+. "Operit does not host chat inference." | source: https://github.com/AAswordman/Operit | publisher: GitHub (official repo) | pub_date: 2026-07-01 (v1.12.0) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: landscape
- claim: **Blurr / Panda** ("Your Personal AI Phone Operator") is an Android app that operates the phone UI through the Accessibility Service. It needs Gemini keys or a proxy backend you supply. It has about 993 stars and a non-OSI **Personal Use License** (commercial use needs a license). It is marked WIP: memory is "temporarily disabled" and the roadmap is "NOT UPDATED". | source: https://github.com/Ayush0Chaudhary/blurr | publisher: GitHub (official repo) | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: landscape
- claim: **Open-AutoGLM** (zai-org) is Apache-2.0 with about 26.6k stars. It is a phone-agent framework plus the AutoGLM-Phone-9B (and Multilingual) VLM. It requires a PC with ADB (or HDC for HarmonyOS); self-hosting the model needs a GPU with 24GB+ VRAM, or you use the hosted Zhipu or ModelScope endpoints. The README says it is for research and learning only, defaults to a 100-step cap, and returns black screenshots on payment and banking screens. | source: https://github.com/zai-org/Open-AutoGLM | publisher: GitHub (official repo) | pub_date: undated (no releases) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: landscape
- claim: **OpenClaw on Android** is a set of community projects, not official. AidanPark/openclaw-android (MIT, about 1.8k stars, pins OpenClaw 2026.10.2) runs the OpenClaw gateway natively in Termux with only glibc ld.so (about 200MB) and also offers a standalone "Claw" APK. It is BYOK to cloud LLMs (OpenAI, Gemini, etc.), runs the gateway and Playwright browser automation, and does not claim to control the phone UI. Its known issues are Android background killing, the Phantom Process Killer on Android 12+, and the need to disable battery optimization. | source: https://github.com/AidanPark/openclaw-android | publisher: GitHub (community repo) | pub_date: undated (pins 2026.10.x) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: high | class: landscape
- claim: There are other OpenClaw-on-Termux variants, such as the openclaw-termux npm fork (APK, no root), proot-distro Ubuntu guides and the irtiq7 installer, plus blog posts on turning "an old Android into a 24/7 AI employee". Termux:API gives access to phone hardware. A Korean guide notes it is officially not recommended. | source: https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/openclaw-termux@1.6.1/README.md ; https://alisolanki.substack.com/p/how-i-turned-an-old-android-into ; https://github.com/irtiq7/OpenClaw-Android | publisher: npm/community blogs | pub_date: 2026 (various) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: landscape
- claim: **Hermes Agent** (Nous Research) is an MIT-licensed, open-source, self-improving agent that appeared around Feb 2026. People run it on a VPS because a laptop host stops the agent when it sleeps, which breaks scheduled and overnight tasks. The VPS only hosts the process, terminal state and memory; inference goes to OpenRouter, Nous Portal or OpenAI, so a $5 server (1 vCPU, 1GB RAM) is enough. Guides warn that an always-on agent with shell access is risky without a non-root user and a firewall. | source: https://www.digitalocean.com/resources/articles/what-is-hermes-agent ; https://madewithlove.com/blog/hermes-the-agent-that-doesnt-quit-when-you-close-your-laptop/ ; https://hermesatlas.com/guide/install/vps/ | publisher: DigitalOcean / madewithlove / Hermes Atlas (secondary) | pub_date: 2026 (various) | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium (no primary Nous source fetched) | class: landscape
- claim: The same "VPS only hosts the loop, inference is remote" pattern holds for OpenClaw. A phone can replace the VPS for the gateway, as openclaw-android shows, but has to work around Android's background-process limits. | source: https://github.com/AidanPark/openclaw-android ; https://hermesatlas.com/guide/install/vps/ | publisher: GitHub / Hermes Atlas | pub_date: undated | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: landscape
- claim (gap assessment): Operit comes closest to "fully on-phone, open-source, BYOK agent with phone control, a terminal for coding, local-model fallback and MCP". It is LGPL-3.0 and actively released (Jul 2026). Its limits: I found no evidence in this run that it is designed for 24/7 always-on operation with scheduled or autonomous tasks, nor that it handles free-tier quotas or rotates free-provider keys. OpenClaw-on-Termux covers always-on but not native phone-UI control. DroidRun and Open-AutoGLM need a PC. Panda is not OSI open source. | source: synthesis of the repo findings above | publisher: — | pub_date: — | accessed: 2026-11-14 | confidence: medium | class: landscape

## Leads (worth chasing, not chased)
- Operit's always-on, scheduling and background behaviour (does it run 24/7 tasks or triggers? check issues, docs and releases after v1.12.0). This is the key differentiation question.
- Google ML Kit "agentic workflows" / `AgentRunner` page: whether third-party apps get on-device tool-calling loops with Gemini Nano, and which devices are supported.
- Vibe Browser blog "building OpenClaw native Android AI agent" (https://www.vibebrowser.app/blog/building-openclaw-native-android-ai-agent) and ClawPhone (hermesatlas.com/projects/marshallrichards/ClawPhone): possible native, always-on Android competitors.
- DroidClaw (in https://themenonlab.blog/blog/ai-agents-android-droidclaw-openclaw-termux): unexamined.
- mobile-use (minitap), AppAgent, Mobile-Agent (X-PLUG), PocketPal, Maid, and Claude Code / Hermes on Termux: not fetched this round.
- Groq Free plan tab numbers (account console or a second source); Gemini free RPD from the AI Studio dashboard for current Flash/Flash-Lite.
- OpenRouter free model churn: a primary changelog or announcement of free model removals.
- AndroidWorld or agent papers that report tokens per task (e.g. M3A, Agent-S, UI-TARS cost tables).
- Official OpenClaw docs: what it is, its license, and official Android/Termux stance.
